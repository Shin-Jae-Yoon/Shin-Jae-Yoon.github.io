# Dev Uni multidisciplinary design direction review

## Status

- Review model: Sol
- Review date: 2026-08-01
- Review lanes: brand strategy, brand marketing, editorial design, web UX, information architecture, visual audit, reference research, adversarial critique
- Decision: **direction approved for a new whole-site visual board; implementation is not approved yet**
- Public brand: **개발자 유니의 두 번째 뇌**
- Internal working label: **Working Archive** — documentation only, never a public-facing slogan

## Executive decision

`SIGNAL / FIELD` is rejected as the public and visual direction. `ARCHIVE OS` is also rejected as a public concept name.

Both proposals solved visual consistency by applying a strong design package. That approach created the same problem in a different style: the system was memorable, but the person and archive were not. The result could still be mistaken for a fashionable agency board or an AI-generated developer portfolio.

The new direction will not begin with a visual trend. It will begin with the site's real identity:

> 현장에서 배우고 공부한 것을 연결해 다시 꺼내 쓰는 개발자의 두 번째 뇌.

The visual system should make the archive, writing, project evidence, note paths, backlinks, dates, graph relationships, portrait, and personal history visible. Real material—not invented decoration—creates authorship.

### Final panel verdict

- Brand, editorial, and web-UX reviewers approved the direction as a basis for visual exploration.
- The adversarial design reviewer rejected immediate implementation because the direction is still too abstract to prevent another generic archive template.
- Therefore the combined verdict is: **approve the direction, reject Terra implementation, require a complete visual board and graph/content specifications first.**
- Internal phrases such as `Working Archive`, `archive entrance`, `map room`, and `evidence dossier` are planning shorthand only and are forbidden in public-facing copy.

## What the reviewers agreed on

1. `개발자 유니의 두 번째 뇌` is already the strongest brand and SEO asset.
2. Another English concept name weakens recognition instead of strengthening it.
3. Home, About, Portfolio, Brain, and Articles need different spatial roles, not one reusable hero template.
4. Brain's explorer and graph are genuine identity assets and should not be reduced to decoration.
5. About should show a person; Portfolio should show decisions and evidence; Articles should feel like a publication index.
6. The existing navy/cyan lineage is worth preserving, but fluorescent or multi-accent styling makes the site feel like a generic design showcase.
7. Responsive layouts must be designed as separate compositions below 1200px, not scaled desktop screens.
8. Implementation should wait until a complete visual board is reviewed across all primary routes.

## Where the reviewers disagreed

| Question | Keep SIGNAL / FIELD | Retire SIGNAL / FIELD | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is its hard grid useful? | Yes. It provides hierarchy and a strong system. | Yes, but it overwhelms authorship when used everywhere. | Keep grid discipline, remove the branded industrial costume. |
| Should its interaction structure survive? | Yes. Stable header, rails, drawer, graph states are strong. | The visual expression is too studio-like. | Preserve IA and behavior; redesign visual grammar. |
| Should cobalt/citron/orange remain? | They create energy and differentiation. | Together they resemble a trend board and compete with content. | Retire the ensemble. Use one mature archive blue and one rare annotation accent. |
| Should the site feel editorial? | Yes. | Yes, but not like a magazine template. | Use editorial rhythm only where content supports it. |

The minority view was useful: the previous proposal's strongest part was not its name or palette, but its clear behavioral system. That structure is retained.

## Evidence from the previous Dev Uni blog

The previous blog is not a visual target to copy literally. It is evidence of the site's authentic DNA:

- deep navy and cyan established a recognizable technical tone;
- the Brain explorer, graph, and reading layout were practical rather than ornamental;
- navigation was direct, especially in the mobile drawer;
- actual notes and categories created density and identity;
- the real portrait and Korean prose felt more personal than generic portfolio modules.

The redesign should mature these qualities. It should not return to the old centered hero, oversized `SECOND BRAIN` treatment, or purely cyan technical-blog styling.

## Recommended direction: Working Archive

`Working Archive` is an internal design description, not a new brand name.

The site should feel like a calm, active workspace where finished articles and unfinished knowledge can coexist. It combines three modes:

- **editorial archive** for Articles;
- **map room** for Brain;
- **evidence dossier** for Portfolio.

Home introduces these modes without presenting a generic landing-page sales pitch.

### Brand position

- Public name: `개발자 유니의 두 번째 뇌`
- Short descriptor: `배우고 연결하고 기록합니다.`
- Positioning sentence: `현장에서 배우고 공부한 것을 연결해 다시 꺼내 쓰는 개발자의 두 번째 뇌.`
- Voice: concrete, curious, calm, technically serious, personally authored

### Visual character

- mineral white and paper surfaces rather than fake paper textures;
- ink navy rather than flat black;
- archive blue evolved from the legacy cyan;
- one rare rust-red annotation accent;
- rules, indexes, paths, dates, image crops, and graph relationships derived from real data;
- controlled asymmetry where content requires it, not decorative randomness;
- no universal card system and no universal giant-title system.

### Proposed core palette

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mineral white | `#F4F3EF` | site field |
| Paper | `#FCFBF7` | reading and data surfaces |
| Ink navy | `#15272C` | primary text and dark fields |
| Archive blue | `#32677A` | links, active states, graph focus |
| Graph mist | `#D8E0DF` | graph edges and quiet rules |
| Archive gray | `#6D7471` | secondary text and metadata |
| Annotation rust | `#B45B48` | rare human annotation and warning |

Rules:

- Archive blue is the only routine interaction color.
- Rust appears rarely and must convey annotation, emphasis, or a real result.
- No cobalt/citron/orange combination, gradients, glass panels, pastel blobs, or fake material effects.
- Dark fields are reserved for Home's opening and selected Portfolio/Brain moments, not every page.

## Page roles

### Home — archive entrance

- A concise opening, not a giant marketing hero.
- Keep the phrase `생각은 연결될 때 오래 남는다.` as the memorable line.
- Show a restrained living connection visual backed by actual Brain relationships.
- Present About, Portfolio, Brain, and Articles as four distinct routes with real counts or recent material.
- Avoid duplicated explanation sections and generic feature cards.

### About — personal essay

- Lead with Shin Jae-yoon as a person, supported by the supplied portrait.
- Tell the transition from electrical engineering to computer science, curiosity, coffee, conversation, and action.
- Keep professional proof in Portfolio.
- Use one composed portrait spread, not a profile card or résumé dashboard.

### Portfolio — evidence dossier

- Cases follow `problem → judgment → implementation → evidence`.
- Multiple company projects and personal projects remain distinct.
- Only verified metrics appear.
- Contact information is useful but secondary to engineering evidence.

### Brain — map room

- Brain landing: persistent Explorer plus a large global graph as the dominant instrument.
- Brain note: Explorer / article / compact graph–TOC–backlink rail.
- Current, directly connected, and unrelated nodes have three clear levels.
- Graph meaning comes from actual links and tags, not from mechanically connecting every note to a Brain root.
- The landing graph may be visually strong; detail graphs stay contextual and compact.
- Graph semantics must distinguish authored note links, backlinks, folders/categories, tags, and current-node context. Do not manufacture visual density by connecting every note directly to `Brain`.

### Articles — publication index

- A ruled, filterable index designed to scale to hundreds of posts.
- Categories, dates, titles, and frontmatter tags remain stable in position and size.
- No hover preview windows and no repeated article cards.

### Reading surfaces

- Article body remains centered at a comfortable measure.
- Brain notes preserve the Explorer; ordinary articles do not.
- Desktop right rail owns local graph, TOC, and backlinks with independent scroll regions.
- TOC anchors account for the sticky header and land in one jump.

## Responsive contract

### 1200px and above

- Stable desktop header.
- Brain notes use three zones: Explorer / article / right rail.
- Rails align to the same sticky offset and sit toward the viewport edges.

### Below 1200px

- Global navigation becomes a drawer.
- Brain Explorer becomes the primary Brain sheet inside the drawer; other routes do not render it.
- TOC sits directly below title and metadata.
- Graph opens as a separate overlay above a neutral scrim.
- Backlinks follow the article or use a compact collapsible region.
- Reading mode removes supporting regions from layout rather than hiding them visually.

## Preserve, mature, discard

### Preserve

- `개발자 유니의 두 번째 뇌` and `/brain`;
- the practical Explorer and graph-navigation model;
- stable header, drawer, sticky rails, graph overlay, TOC, backlinks, and back-to-top behavior;
- Articles category index;
- real portrait, biography, résumé evidence, migrated prose, dates, tags, and note paths;
- the legacy navy/cyan lineage, matured into ink navy and archive blue.

### Mature

- turn technical density into deliberate archive rhythm;
- use real metadata instead of decorative labels;
- let each route own a distinct composition;
- let graph scale reflect context: dominant on Brain landing, compact on detail pages;
- design mobile and tablet behavior independently below 1200px.

### Discard

- `SIGNAL / FIELD`, `ARCHIVE OS`, and other public English meta-concepts;
- electric cobalt, citron, and orange as a palette ensemble;
- giant typography repeated on every route;
- fake paper textures, stamps, scribbles, crop marks, or index cards used only for atmosphere;
- generic AI-style cards, floating dashboards, unsupported metrics, gradients, glass, and 3D decoration;
- abstract slogans that are not supported by actual site material.

## Three possible visual variants

### A. Working Archive — recommended

Light mineral field, ink typography, mature archive blue, real metadata, and differentiated page compositions. It best balances the legacy blog, personal authorship, long-form reading, and a modern finish.

### B. Technical Journal

Stronger publication rhythm and selective serif accents. It could improve Articles, but risks making the entire site feel like a magazine rather than a working knowledge system.

### C. Workshop Interface

Darker, more utilitarian, and closest to the old blog's technical identity. It suits Brain, but using it everywhere risks another developer-tool cliché.

Final recommendation: **A as the site-wide base, with C used only inside Brain's map-room moments.**

## Anti-AI acceptance tests

1. The Korean brand is visible without an invented English concept name.
2. Home, About, Portfolio, Brain, and Articles are identifiable in grayscale by composition alone.
3. Real archive data determines visible structure.
4. Replacing the name and content with another developer's should visibly break the design's meaning.
5. No universal hero, card grid, graph motif, or accent treatment is reused across every route.
6. The 390px and 1024px compositions look intentionally designed rather than collapsed desktop pages.
7. The site remains readable and useful when motion and color are removed.
